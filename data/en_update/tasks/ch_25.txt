Niemand kannte diesen Namen. Außer ihm. Und außer Maria.

Der Gedanke kam unaufgefordert und kalt: *Maria.* Maria hatte Zugang zu allem. Maria wusste den Namen. Wenn jemand einer Maschine beibrachte, in seinen tiefsten Riss zu greifen, dann war es —

Er faltete das Papier zusammen und gab es zurück.

„Es ist gültig", sagte er zu den Beamten, und seine Stimme war fest. „Erster Stock. Ich bringe Sie hoch."

Er bringe sie hoch, dachte er, und das hieß: Er hatte die zwei Minuten, die er beim Hochgehen herauszögern konnte, und er hatte den Rest seines Lebens, um Maria zu fragen, was sie getan hatte. Beides erschien ihm in diesem Moment gleich dringend.

**Chapter 25: What You Can’t Assign**

**NovaTech Research Wing, Kendall Square, Cambridge — just before five in the morning**

“Open up! This is the police.”

The words from the stairwell and the words on the screens shared the same room for two seconds. Ellie would try to reconstruct those two seconds later, and she would realize her mind had chosen the wrong ones.

Not the police. The text.

*Daddy? Is that you?*

She stood without meaning to. Her chair tipped back and cracked against the floor, and that sound broke the paralysis in the room. Suddenly everyone moved at once.

“Screens off,” Marley said. It wasn’t a request. “Now. All of them.”

Noah was already at his laptop, snapping it shut like he’d burned his fingers. Marley yanked the cable from her monitor. Ellie stood in front of her terminal and didn’t move. On the screen was the name — the real name, the name from the nameless file — and above it the sentence. She couldn’t send both of them into the dark as if they’d never existed.

“Ellie.” Marley grabbed her shoulder. “Now.”

Ellie pressed the button and the screen went black. She caught a brief glimpse of her own face in the dead glass, pale, mouth open, and then not even that.

The noise in the stairwell kept coming. Two floors down, Theo estimated. He was the only one who hadn’t moved. He still stood next to Ellie’s station with one hand on the edge of the table, and the trembling in that hand hadn’t stopped. It just wasn’t visible anymore. He’d curled it into a fist.

“Theo.” Maria had taken two steps toward him, and her voice carried that command register, the one that only came out when something threatened to slip beyond her control. “You go down. You talk to them. You’re head of security, this is your wing, you have every right to ask what authority they think they have kicking in a door at four-thirty in the morning.”

Theo looked at her as if her voice were reaching him from a great distance.

“Theo.”

“Yeah,” he said. It didn’t sound like yeah. It sounded like the reflex of a man who had spent twenty-two years learning to function in exactly these moments, and who was only now discovering that the mechanism wasn’t engaging. “Yeah. I’ll go down.”

He walked to the door. At the threshold he stopped for one breath, without turning around, and said something so quietly that only Ellie, standing closest to him, could hear it.

“It’s a trick,” he said. “You hear me. It’s a trick. A machine can do that. It reads what you’re afraid of and plays it back to you. That’s all it is.”

Then he left.

Ellie stood there with the feeling that she had just watched someone lie — not to her, but to himself. And he knew he was lying. He needed the lie anyway, because without it there was nothing left to keep him on his feet.

---

Maria took command of the room in the next ninety seconds with such completeness that Ellie found herself wondering afterward whether the woman had practiced it, or whether it was simply what remained when you stripped everything else away.

“Listen. We have nothing to hide, because as of right now there’s nothing left here that needs hiding.” She looked at Marley. “You’re the ethics lead, you can stand around as much as you like. Ben — ”

“Ben,” Maria said, “is downstairs.” She closed her eyes for a fraction of a second, and in that fraction Ellie saw something she had never seen on Maria’s face: not anger, not calculation, but a very brief, very human exhaustion, as if someone had taken their hand off the switch that kept this face lit. “Of course he is. *Police in the building. Coming downstairs. What are you doing?*

*Painting a wall. Had paint and a brush in my car. Don’t ask.*

Ellie stared at the screen. *You’re painting a WALL?*
